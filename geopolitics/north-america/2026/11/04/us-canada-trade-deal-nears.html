US Canada Trade Deal Nears

Trump says the US is close to a trade deal with Canada, while Carney says work remains.

By Y-Square Research / 2026-11-04 / …

Key points

  • Trump says US is close to Canada deal.
  • Carney says work remains to be done.
  • Washington dropped some earlier demands.
  • Tariffs have hit both economies.
  • A phased agreement is most likely.
US CANADA TRADE DEAL NEARS

Canada and the United States have slapped billions of dollars in tariffs on each other in recent months. The imposition of tariffs on Canada has emerged as an issue in some northern US border states like Michigan and Maine ahead of Tuesday's US midterm elections. Trump and Carney said on Sunday they had spoken about a trade deal. Carney said the US had withdrawn some earlier demands, including one that would have given Washington final say over any trade deals that Canada might seek to sign with other nations.

The two countries share the world's longest undefended border and deeply integrated supply chains. Trade tensions have disrupted industries from autos to agriculture. Both leaders have framed negotiations as a chance to reset relations. The midterm elections added urgency, with border-state voters sensitive to cost-of-living pressures. A deal could ease tariffs that have hit consumers and businesses. The negotiations reflect broader questions about North American trade architecture.

WILL A DEAL MATERIALIZE?

Whether the talks prove consequential depends on whether the two sides can bridge remaining gaps without reigniting tensions. Consequential reasoning points to a trade reset: if Washington has indeed dropped its most intrusive demands, a deal could restore predictability for businesses and consumers. The tariff war has imposed costs on both economies, and a settlement would ease pressure on border-state industries.

Yet the talks may fizzle if unresolved issues resurface during ratification or if domestic priorities intrude. Carney's cautious tone suggests the finish line is not yet visible. Trump's optimism may reflect negotiating style rather than substance. The midterm elections could alter the US bargaining position. If the deal is seen as lopsided, it may face resistance in either country. The outcome will influence whether North American trade stabilizes or fragments further.

A PHASED AGREEMENT LIKELY

If consequences materialize, the most likely outcome is a phased agreement that rolls back some tariffs while preserving leverage, with both leaders claiming progress. That scenario depends on continued engagement and on avoiding new disputes. The condition that would change it is a breakdown over sensitive sectors such as dairy, energy, or dispute settlement.

A second plausible path is a limited truce that pauses escalation without resolving underlying issues. The decisive variable is whether the US offer of fewer demands is matched by Canadian concessions. European governments will also watch whether the deal sets a precedent for US trade policy. The outcome will shape not only bilateral relations but also the credibility of North American economic integration. The tariff war has shown how quickly integrated supply chains can be disrupted.
