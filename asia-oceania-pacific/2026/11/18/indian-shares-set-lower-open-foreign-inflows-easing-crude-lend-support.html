Indian shares face pullback after nine-month high rally

Indian shares may open lower after a nine-month high, with record foreign inflows and easing oil prices likely to limit losses.

By Y-Squre Research / 2026-11-18 / …

Key points

  • GIFT Nifty futures pointed to a lower open after the Nifty 50 closed at 25,387.4 on Tuesday.
  • Foreign portfolio investors bought Indian shares for a 12th consecutive session, with November inflows at a record $10 billion.
  • The Nifty and BSE Sensex have gained 10.1% and 10.8% respectively so far in November.
  • Brent crude fell 0.5% to $92.7 a barrel, extending a 21% decline so far this month.
  • India may limit leveraged stock bets, keeping exchange operators and brokerages in focus.

What Set Up the Nine-month High

Indian shares have climbed sharply into mid-November, with the Nifty 50 and BSE Sensex up 10.1% and 10.8% respectively so far in the month. Foreign portfolio investors have driven much of the move, pouring a record $10 billion into Indian equities in November, according to provisional exchange data. The pace of buying has been unusually persistent, with FPIs buying shares for a 12th consecutive session on Tuesday.

The rally has coincided with a retreat in crude prices. Brent futures have fallen 21% so far this month, easing input-cost concerns for India, a large energy importer. Traders are weighing tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz against a projected supply surplus from recovering Middle East exports, which has kept oil volatile even as the broader trend has favoured buyers of Indian assets.

Global technology sentiment has also helped. Nvidia's better-than-expected sales forecast lifted companies in its hardware supply chain and pushed MSCI's Asia-Pacific ex-Japan index up 0.9% on Tuesday. That regional move gave Indian brokers a calmer backdrop after four days of losses across the broader benchmark.

At home, regulation is the wild card. A Reuters report that India may limit leveraged stock bets to curb volatility kept exchange operators and brokerages in focus. Jefferies said it does not see the plan as a material risk for brokers, exchanges and depositories, because brokerage firms had already curtailed exposure to less liquid stocks. IIFL Finance also drew attention on reports that Canada's Fairfax Financial plans to sell its nearly 11% stake in the non-bank lender.

Wednesday's Open and the Flows Behind It

GIFT Nifty futures were at 25,253 points as of 7:57 a.m. IST on Wednesday, pointing to a negative open for the Nifty 50 after it closed at 25,387.4 on Tuesday. The gap between futures and the cash market is small, but it signals that traders expect a pause after the recent run.

The setup is unusually strong for a pullback. FPIs bought Indian shares worth 41.95 billion rupees ($448.81 million) on Tuesday, marking their 12th consecutive session of net buying, provisional data showed. Foreign inflows have been the dominant demand source as domestic institutions stayed cautious after the rally.

Analysts see consolidation rather than a trend break. Pabitro Mukherjee, deputy vice president of research at Bajaj Broking, said some consolidation is warranted after a 13% rally in just 12 sessions. His caution reflects the speed of the move rather than a change in the inflow story.

Oil could limit the downside. Brent crude futures fell 0.5% to $92.7 per barrel on Wednesday, extending a monthly decline that has eased India's import bill outlook. A softer crude price reduces inflation pressure and preserves corporate margins, which supports the case that selling may be shallow.

Whether the Pullback Changes the Rally's Trajectory

The consequential case rests on foreign money. Record FPI inflows, a weaker crude price and improving regional technology sentiment give Indian equities several independent supports. If those forces hold, a lower open would be a pause inside a broader uptrend rather than the start of a reversal.

The opposite case is also credible. A 13% rally in 12 sessions leaves little room for disappointment, and any hint that foreign buyers are slowing could trigger profit-taking. Regulatory moves to limit leverage could weigh on brokers, exchanges and high-turnover strategies even if Jefferies sees limited fundamental damage.

Cause and coincidence need separating. Nvidia's forecast helped the regional index, but it does not determine Indian inflows. Likewise, falling oil prices assist India's terms of trade, but they do not guarantee that foreign funds will keep buying at the same pace. The rally's next leg depends on whether inflows continue, not on the fact that several positive stories appeared together.

The Strait of Hormuz is the main external risk. Tanker attacks could disrupt supply and push crude back up, which would squeeze India's import costs and revive inflation concerns. For now, traders appear to be pricing a supply surplus, but that assumption can change quickly if the attacks escalate.

The Most Likely Path for Indian Equities

The most likely path is modest consolidation near the recent high. Foreign inflows and softer crude should cushion declines, while the speed of the rally and the leverage debate cap upside. An open slightly lower, followed by a narrow range, fits the balance of forces better than a sharp break.

That outcome depends on two conditions: FPIs keep buying, and Brent stays below the levels that would revive inflation pressure. If either fails, the pullback could deepen, especially in brokers, exchanges and rate-sensitive financials.

For readers, the signal to watch is the continuation of foreign buying after the open. A lower start absorbed by buyers would confirm that the rally is pausing rather than ending. More regional market analysis is available from Y-Square.
