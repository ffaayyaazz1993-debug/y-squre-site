Manila Quad Talks Open a Week of High-Stakes Summits

Trump opens a Manila summit week with Quad talks, then APEC and a three-way meeting with China and Russia, against a fragile truce and new chip curbs.

By Y-Squre Research / 2026-11-17 / …

Key points

  • Trump began his Asia trip in Manila at the close of the ASEAN summit, skipping the East Asia Summit while attending a US-ASEAN Special Summit.
  • The Quad meeting is the first gathering of the US, Japan, Australia and India in two years, held just before talks with China and Russia.
  • Republican midterm losses and the Nvidia chip case have raised the stakes for a fragile US-China trade truce.
  • Protesters gathered in Manila against the visit, calling for an end to the Middle East war and the scrapping of the Pax Silica initiative.
  • A joint statement with Thailand on a reciprocal trade agreement is expected during the trip.

Manila Arrival after the Midterm Reversals

Donald Trump arrives in Manila at the close of this week's Association of Southeast Asian Nations gathering, carrying a compressed schedule and a weaker hand than a year ago. His Republican Party lost ground in the midterm elections, and a spy case involving Nvidia's Jensen Huang has turned the technology relationship with Beijing into an immediate test. Those two developments frame every conversation on this trip, from the four-party session to the bilateral meeting with Xi Jinping.

The president has chosen to skip the annual East Asia Summit, a departure that indicates where his attention lies. He will attend a US-ASEAN Special Summit instead and meet Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr, a defence ally, before travelling to China for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum. Analysts in Beijing expect him to pursue agreements that can be presented as wins at home, given the scale of the electoral reversal.

The setting helps explain the tight itinerary. ASEAN brings eleven economies together with combined output above $3.9 trillion, and China remains its largest trading partner.

Several bigger members want deeper commercial ties with Washington even while the diplomatic relationship stays unsettled. Premier Li Qiang used Monday's meeting to praise China-ASEAN performance and to urge free trade, language that sits awkwardly beside restrictions under discussion in Washington.

Protesters have gathered in Manila ahead of the arrival, burning effigies and demanding an end to the Middle East war and to the US-led Pax Silica initiative. The demonstrations add a visible constraint to a visit shaped by the midterm results.

QUAD Revival before the Three-way Meeting

Trump's first event is the Quad gathering, the first meeting of the four leaders in two years. The group brings together the United States, Japan, Australia and India, and the previous administration treated it as a signature undertaking.

Beijing has long described the format as an attempt at encirclement, while members insist it targets no single country. The session comes immediately before Thursday's talks involving Trump, Xi and Russia's Vladimir Putin.

Former US deputy secretary of state Kurt Campbell credited Secretary of State Marco Rubio with arranging the revival. He argued that the timing sends the strongest signal before the three-way conversation, and warned that any impression Washington had lowered its commitment to Japan would be troublesome.

The White House called the Quad a partnership of action. Japanese officials expect discussion of maritime security, export controls, critical-mineral supply chains and artificial intelligence.

On the sidelines, Trump will meet Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul, with a joint statement expected on a reciprocal trade agreement. The United States buys about a quarter of Thai shipments this year, and designated Thailand a major non-NATO ally in 2003.

A transcript released with the reporting warns that automated speech conversion may contain errors. That caveat matters for remarks attributed to unnamed analysts, which read as commentary rather than official positions.

Chip Restrictions and the Fragility of the Truce

Whether the talks produce durable results depends on two variables that sit outside the summit room. The first is congressional pressure over advanced chips. The House speaker has indicated a growing willingness in Congress to limit Beijing's access to high-end processors, a shift that analysts in China fear could embolden harder-line voices and disturb an already delicate trade truce.

The second is the scope of what Trump can offer. Analysts describe him as weaker after the midterms and inclined to secure visible outcomes, with the wars in Ukraine and Iran also on his list to address. That creates an incentive to trade concessions across several files at once, which raises the chance of an agreement announced with more precision than substance.

Against that, the structural incentives run the other way. China is deeply embedded in ASEAN supply chains, and the regional economies represented in Manila have little appetite for a fresh round of restrictions.

The joint statement expected with Thailand points toward a narrower, transactional style of deal-making that can survive without a broad settlement. Co-occurrence of a summit and a truce does not establish that the one sustains the other.

The most probable result is a set of modest, concrete deliverables rather than a sweeping arrangement. Expect a restated commitment to the four-party format, a joint statement with Thailand on tariff terms, and carefully worded language from the trilateral that avoids firm promises on either Ukraine or Iran. Each of these can be presented as progress without requiring either side to retreat from its stated position.

A Narrow Set of Summit Deliverables

That outcome depends on one condition holding: that neither the chip case nor the Manila protests escalate into a confrontational moment during the visit. If congressional action on export controls advances while the summit is underway, the truce language will become harder to sustain, and the Chinese delegation will have less room to appear constructive. The trilateral would then produce communique language rather than commitments.
